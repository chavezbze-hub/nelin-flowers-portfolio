Licensure preparation

New York’s LMSW pathway follows graduate social work education, so my first step after the B.S.W. is to pursue the appropriate M.S.W. route and verify current requirements with NYSED.

  1. During the B.S.W.
    Complete coursework and field learning, keep a record of training and supervision, and discuss licensure planning with my academic advisor.
  2. After graduation
    Compare CSWE-accredited M.S.W. programs and confirm whether my B.S.W. qualifies for advanced-standing consideration.
  3. After the M.S.W.
    Review the current NYSED application, education, examination, and registration requirements before applying for the LMSW.
  4. Long-term
    If clinical practice remains my goal, pursue supervised post-M.S.W. experience and review the current LCSW requirements.

07 / Advocacy in practice

Supporting informed choices through a complex system.

A personal advocacy reflection that connects lived experience to my developing commitment to dignity, self-determination, and access.

Personal advocacy reflection

I supported a friend who was navigating a complex vehicle-financing and lease-termination dispute. I listened to her concerns, helped organize relevant information, encouraged her to ask questions, and supported her in seeking appropriate consumer and financial guidance. I was concerned that age may have influenced how she was treated, while recognizing that my role was to support her communication and decision-making—not to determine the legal merits of the situation.

The matter ultimately reached a favorable resolution for her: the company repurchased the vehicle and placed her in a new lease for the same make and model at a slightly lower payment. This experience strengthened my commitment to self-determination, informed decision-making, dignity, and advocacy when individuals are navigating complicated systems.

Details are intentionally generalized to protect privacy. This is a personal advocacy experience, not formal social work or legal practice.
